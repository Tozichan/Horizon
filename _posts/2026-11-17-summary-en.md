### [Shandong University team claims first conditional proof of Goldbach conjecture](https://mrc.sdu.edu.cn/info/1018/2193.htm) ⭐️ 9.0/10

A Shandong University team, Li Jiamin and Liu Jianya, has posted a paper claiming the first conditional proof of the binary Goldbach conjecture covering all sufficiently large even numbers: every such even number is the sum of two primes lying between one-third and two-thirds of it. The result depends on the Generalized Riemann Hypothesis for Dirichlet L-functions plus one of two additional spectral assumptions, and the authors state that GRH alone is insufficient. The paper says the method differs from classical sieve and circle methods. The claim is conditional and, per the source, not yet independently verified.

telegram · zaihuapd · Nov 16, 16:01

**「Background」** The binary Goldbach conjecture states that every even integer greater than 2 is the sum of two primes; it remains unproven. The generalized Riemann hypothesis \(GRH\) extends the Riemann hypothesis—that the nontrivial zeros of the Riemann zeta function have real part 1/2—to a broader class of L-functions, including the Dirichlet L-functions the Shandong University paper invokes. The team&\#x27;s result is explicitly conditional: it assumes GRH plus one of two additional spectral hypotheses, and the authors state that GRH alone does not suffice.

**「What it means」** Because the proof rests on unproven hypotheses, it does not establish Goldbach unconditionally; its value depends on whether the two spectral assumptions can be verified or replaced, and on independent checking of the argument. Readers should treat the &quot;first&quot; claim as the authors&\#x27; assertion rather than a confirmed result.

### [duetto steers a pretrained music transformer at sampling time for live jazz](https://www.reddit.com/r/MachineLearning/comments/1xxkyh3/steering_a_pretrained_music_transformer_at/) ⭐️ 7.0/10

A project called duetto provides a real-time jazz accompaniment partner for a MIDI keyboard, playing standards as a small band \(solo, comping, walking bass\), comping while the player plays and soloing when they stop. Rather than fine-tuning, it uses the pretrained Anticipatory Music Transformer \(Thickstun et al., 2023\) as-is and controls generation at sampling time: the player&\#x27;s and model&\#x27;s notes form one event sequence, and before each sample a rule layer masks disallowed logits \(a hand that must wait, out-of-register pitches, off-beat bass notes\) and biases likely ones \(current chord tones, a guide tone on the downbeat, the next note of a quoted phrase\). Phrases come from the Weimar Jazz Database, segmented at breaths, transposed to the tune&\#x27;s chords and developed over a chorus; in the Autumn Leaves demo at 160 bpm, about 61% of solo notes come from quoted phrases or the theme. The author reports roughly 12 ms/note \(medium\) and 18 ms/note \(large\) on a 3080 Ti, with the large model at ~90 ms/note on a Jetson Orin Nano, and notes the system can drift from the player and that the balance between model ideas and rules is fragile.

reddit · r/MachineLearning · /u/GGAllin43 · Nov 16, 09:19

**「Background」** The Anticipatory Music Transformer, published by Thickstun et al. in 2023, is an autoregressive model trained to continue an event sequence while treating some events as controls, which lets it perform infilling tasks such as generating an accompaniment to a given melody. Its authors report that it matches prompted-generation baselines and that human evaluators rated its 20-second accompaniments as similar in musicality to human-composed ones. Duetto builds on that pretrained model without retraining it, adding rule-based logit edits at sampling time.

**「Practical takeaway」** The project suggests sampling-time logit masking and biasing can substitute for fine-tuning in a latency-sensitive symbolic-music setting, with the large model still fast enough for live play on a Jetson Orin Nano. The author&\#x27;s stated limitations—drift from the player, unpredictable responses, and a fragile bias balance—are the main concerns for anyone adapting the approach; the code is free for noncommercial use.
